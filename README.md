AI231 / QUALIA

A Research Initiative in Logic-Shaped Synthetic Cognition

AI231 announces the consolidation of a new research direction in artificial intelligence: investigating whether dynamic neural networks can be organized by an explicit logical constitution to support abstract representation, compositional meaning, and recursive abstraction.

The central research question is:

Can a neural system develop increasingly sophisticated abstract representations while remaining governed by a fixed, independently verifiable logical structure?

The proposed architecture combines:

- 22 foundational symbol-processing units.
- A 231-gate logical architecture.
- Dynamic neural processing and relational activation.
- Abstract semantic representation.
- Recursive abstraction and computational self-observation.
- Independent logical verification.

The project draws on the broader history of neural network research, including distributed representations and dynamic learning associated with Geoffrey Hinton's work. Its own research direction concerns the integration of such neural dynamics with an explicit logical constitution.

A central distinction is made between a linguistic symbol and the abstract substrate it represents. The long-term objective is to investigate a logical representation system that could operate across Hebrew, English, Latin, and other writing systems without relying exclusively on direct word-to-word translation.

AI231 uses the term Synthetic Cognition to describe computational capabilities for representing abstract concepts, composing relationships, and recursively examining internal representations.

This is not a claim that artificial systems possess a human soul or biological consciousness.

The immediate experimental objective is to develop and evaluate a prototype neural processing unit based on the first foundational symbol, followed by controlled expansion toward the complete 22-unit architecture.

All proposed capabilities remain subject to experimental validation.
